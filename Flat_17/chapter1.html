✦ Chapter 1 ✦

Flat 17

✦

Moving to Ravenfall was supposed to fix everything.

New city.New job.New life.

That’s what I told myself while sitting in the backseat of a taxi at nearly eleven o’clock at night, staring at the apartment building through rain-covered windows.

The building looked horrifying.

Not “old but cute” horrifying.

Actual horror movie horrifying.

Eight floors of cracked walls, flickering lights, and dark windows staring down at me like dead eyes.

A giant neon sign above the entrance buzzed weakly.

BLACKTHORNE APARTMENTS

One letter kept flickering on and off.

Cute.

Absolutely adorable.

“Miss?” the taxi driver asked impatiently. “You getting out or planning to live in my car?”

I blinked.

Right.

I quickly grabbed my bags and stepped outside into the freezing rain.

The cold hit instantly.

My hoodie was soaked within seconds as I dragged my suitcase toward the entrance.

Three weeks ago, I was still living comfortably with my parents.

Now?

I was moving into the cheapest apartment I could find because apparently “dream jobs” don’t pay enough to survive in expensive cities.

Still.

Assistant editor at Holloway Publishing was worth it.

At least that’s what Kate kept telling me.

My phone buzzed inside my pocket.

Speak of the devil.

Kate

I answered immediately while struggling with my suitcase.

“Please tell me you didn’t die already,” Kate said dramatically.

I snorted softly.

“Unfortunately, I survived.”

“Describe the apartment.”

I looked up at the building again.

“It looks like a serial killer’s favorite vacation spot.”

Kate burst out laughing.

“I TOLD you not to choose the cheapest option!”

“Okay rich girl, some of us enjoy having money for food.”

“Eris, I’m serious. You said the reviews were weird.”

I rolled my eyes while pushing open the lobby doors.

“People online are dramatic.”

The second I stepped inside, my confidence disappeared slightly.

The lobby was unnaturally quiet.

The lights buzzed softly overhead.

Everything smelled like old wood and dust.

An old man sat behind the reception desk reading a newspaper.

Slowly…

He looked up at me.

And stared.

Not normal staring.

The uncomfortable kind.

Kate’s voice crackled through the phone.

“Eris?”

“Yeah,” I muttered quietly. “I think the building manager might secretly be a vampire.”

The old man’s eyebrows twitched.

Oops.

He stood slowly before handing me a silver key.

“Apartment 18,” he said.

I nodded politely.

“Thank you.”

I turned toward the elevator.

Then his voice stopped me.

“Miss.”

Something about his tone made me pause.

Slowly, I looked back.

The old man hesitated for a moment before speaking.

“Keep your curtains closed after midnight.”

Silence.

Kate immediately went,“What?!”

I laughed awkwardly.

“Uh… okay?”

But the old man had already sat back down.

Conversation over.

“Eris,” Kate said carefully, “why does that sound like the beginning of a Netflix documentary?”

“I hate you.”

“You love me.”

Unfortunately true.

The elevator ride to the eighth floor felt like a near-death experience.

The lights flickered constantly.

Metal creaked loudly around me.

At one point the elevator literally shook.

“If I disappear,” I told Kate, “tell my mother I died hot.”

Kate wheezed laughing through the phone.

Finally—

ding.

The doors opened.

The hallway outside was dim and freezing cold.

Most of the lights barely worked.

My footsteps echoed softly as I dragged my suitcase down the corridor.

Apartment 18.

Right beside it—

Flat 17.

I slowed unconsciously.

The black door looked older than the others.

Deep scratches covered the lower half.

Almost like someone had clawed at it repeatedly.

A weird feeling settled heavily in my chest.

“Eris?” Kate called. “Why’d you stop talking?”

“Nothing.”

I quickly unlocked my apartment and stepped inside.

The place was small but decent enough.

Tiny kitchen.

One bedroom.

Old wooden floors.

And a huge window facing the apartment building directly across from mine.

Rain poured endlessly outside while city lights glowed faintly below.

Honestly?

The view was beautiful.

Maybe this wouldn’t be so bad after all.

After unpacking for hours, exhaustion finally hit me.

By midnight, I sat curled up near the window eating instant ramen while Kate complained about her toxic ex-boyfriend for the hundredth time.

Then suddenly—

A light switched on in the apartment directly opposite mine.

My eyes lifted automatically.

And froze.

Someone sat beside the window across from me.

A boy.

Tall.

Dressed completely in black.

His face remained hidden in shadows, but I could still feel it.

He was staring directly at me.

Not casually.

Not accidentally.

Watching me.

My stomach tightened instantly.

The ramen suddenly tasted like cardboard.

“Kate,” I said slowly.

“Mm?”

“There’s a guy staring at me.”

“What?”

“He’s in the apartment across from mine.”

Kate immediately sounded interested.

“Hot or murderer?”

“I genuinely can’t tell.”

The boy didn’t move.

Didn’t look away.

Just sat there silently watching me like he’d been waiting for me to notice him.

Something about it felt deeply unsettling.

My fingers slowly tightened around the curtain beside me.

“Okay nope,” Kate said instantly. “Close the curtains right now.”

I nodded slightly.

But before I could—

The boy tilted his head slowly.

Almost curiously.

My heartbeat stumbled.

Then suddenly—

The lights inside his apartment went out completely.

Darkness swallowed the entire room.

The chair near the window was empty now.

Gone.

“What happened?” Kate asked quickly.

“I—”

My phone vibrated in my hand.

Unknown Number.

A new message appeared.

Welcome to Blackthorne Apartments, Eris.

— End of Chapter 1 —
