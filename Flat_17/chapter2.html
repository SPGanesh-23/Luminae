Keep The Curtains Closed

✦

My heart stopped.

I stared at the message on my screen while cold fear slowly crept beneath my skin.

Welcome to Blackthorne Apartments, Eris.

“Eris?” Kate’s voice crackled through the phone. “What happened?”

“I just got a message.”

“What kind of message?”

I swallowed hard.

“From an unknown number.”

Silence.

Then—

“Oh my god,” Kate whispered dramatically. “It’s the murder guy.”

“You are not helping.”

“Read it.”

I hesitated before quietly reading the message out loud.

For a second, neither of us spoke.

Then Kate immediately said,“How does he even have your number?”

“I don’t know.”

But even while saying it, uneasiness twisted inside my stomach.

Because the timing felt wrong.

Too wrong.

Almost like he’d been waiting for me to notice him.

Another message appeared before I could think further.

Close your curtains, Eris.

My breath caught.

Slowly, my eyes lifted toward the dark apartment across from mine.

Nothing.

No lights.

No movement.

Just darkness staring back at me through the rain.

Still…

I quickly grabbed the curtains and shut them tightly.

“Yeah,” Kate said immediately. “Nope. Block him.”

I looked down at the number again.

Something about it felt strange.

Not playful.

Not random.

Personal.

But eventually, I pressed block anyway.

“There,” Kate said proudly. “Problem solved.”

I nodded slowly.

Except somehow…

It didn’t feel solved at all.

Nearly an hour later, Kate finally hung up after making me promise not to “die mysteriously on my first night.”

The apartment became painfully quiet afterward.

Rain tapped softly against the windows.

Pipes rattled occasionally somewhere inside the walls.

And every tiny sound made me more aware that I was completely alone.

I tried distracting myself by unpacking more boxes.

Didn’t work.

The creepy message kept replaying inside my head.

Close your curtains, Eris.

I hated how unsettled it made me feel.

Around 1:30 AM, exhaustion finally started winning.

I changed into pajamas, tied my hair up messily, and headed toward the kitchen for water.

That’s when someone knocked on my door.

I froze instantly.

Three slow knocks.

My heart started pounding.

Nobody should be visiting this late.

Another knock echoed through the apartment.

Okay.

Maybe it was the owner uncle.

Or someone complaining about noise.

Still nervous, I slowly walked toward the door and checked through the peephole.

Relief hit immediately.

It was the old man from downstairs.

I opened the door carefully.

“Oh,” I breathed out awkwardly. “You scared me.”

The old man looked slightly uncomfortable standing there.

In his hands was a small flashlight.

“The power cuts happen sometimes during storms,” he explained quietly. “Just checking if everything’s alright.”

For the first time since arriving, I relaxed slightly.

“Oh. Yeah, I’m fine.”

His eyes briefly wandered behind me into the apartment.

Then toward the closed curtains.

A strange expression crossed his face.

“You closed them,” he murmured.

Something about the way he said it made my stomach twist again.

“Um… yeah.”

Silence.

The hallway lights flickered overhead.

The old man looked strangely tense for a second before speaking again.

“If you hear noises from next door,” he said slowly, “ignore them.”

I blinked.

“Next door?”

His eyes shifted toward Flat 17 beside my apartment.

Then back to me.

“The walls are old,” he muttered. “Sounds travel.”

Before I could ask anything else, he suddenly gave a short nod.

“Goodnight, Miss Eris.”

And walked away down the hallway.

I stared after him in confusion before slowly closing the door again.

Weird.

Everything about this building was weird.

I locked the door carefully and leaned against it with a sigh.

At least I felt calmer now.

Maybe I was overthinking everything.

Maybe the message really was just some random creep trying to scare the new girl.

Yeah.

That had to be it.

Trying to shake off the anxiety, I walked back toward the kitchen.

Then stopped.

My phone screen lit up silently on the counter.

Unknown Number.

My blood turned cold.

Because I blocked him.

Hands trembling slightly, I opened the message.

You shouldn’t open the door so easily, Eris.

Another message appeared instantly.

Especially at night.

My heartbeat slowed painfully.

Then the final message came.

What if next time it isn’t him?
