7:43 AM — Westwood High

The rain had finally stopped.

Not completely.

Just enough that the sky looked exhausted instead of angry.

Students flooded through the front entrance carrying backpacks, coffees, unfinished homework, and varying levels of regret about being awake.

Ethan Parker walked through the crowd the same way he always did.

Quietly.

Headphones around his neck.

Phone in one hand.

Scrolling reels.

A dog trying to fit into a shoebox.

Someone attempting a backflip and immediately regretting it.

A cooking video he watched for twelve seconds before realizing he wasn't actually paying attention.

Then—

His phone buzzed.

Instagram notification.

Ethan glanced down.

Then stopped walking.

For a second he genuinely thought he'd read it wrong.

chloeh_ mentioned you in her story.

He blinked.

Looked again.

Still there.

His thumb tapped the notification.

The story opened.

A picture of a slice of honey almond cake.

Warm lighting.

Mabel's table.

The exact cake from yesterday.

Caption:

still thinking about this.

Tagged beneath it:

@ethan_p

Ethan stared.

People flowed around him in the hallway.

Someone bumped his shoulder.

He barely noticed.

Because Chloe Hopper had just voluntarily tagged him in an Instagram story.

His phone vibrated again.

And again.

And again.

Messages were already appearing.

bro???

WAIT YOU KNOW CHLOE HOPPER???

since when???

ETHAN???

One particularly dramatic message simply said:

WHAT.

Ethan rubbed a hand over his face.

He opened the story again.

Looked at the mention.

Looked at the repost button.

Closed it.

Opened it again.

Closed it.

Because reposting it felt weird.

But not reposting it felt weirder.

Eventually he sighed.

Pressed the button.

Added nothing.

No caption.

No emoji.

Just reposted it.

Immediately regretted it.

Then kept walking.

By the time he reached Classroom 214 he already knew what was waiting for him.

The hallway outside buzzed louder than usual.

And unfortunately—

Several people were looking directly at him.

Not through him.

At him.

Which was significantly worse.

"...that's him."

"No way."

"Seriously?"

"I've literally never heard him talk."

"He's in our class, right?"

"I think so?"

"You THINK so?"

Ethan closed his eyes briefly.

Wonderful.

Someone near the door actually frowned while looking at him.

"Damn."

"What?"

"I genuinely forgot he was in our class."

The guy sounded apologetic about it.

Which somehow made it worse.

Ethan walked inside.

The room was already full.

Conversations everywhere.

Phones out.

People pretending they weren't staring.

He headed automatically toward his usual seat by the windows.

Then stopped.

Because someone was sitting there.

Chloe.

She looked up from her phone.

"Morning."

A few nearby conversations immediately died.

Ethan glanced around.

Half the class suddenly seemed fascinated by their notebooks.

"Morning."

Chloe tapped the empty seat beside her.

"Claire's sick."

"...Okay?"

"You can sit there."

The entire row went silent.

A basketball player across the room stared.

"Wait."

He looked between them.

"You guys know each other?"

Chloe didn't even look up.

"Since yesterday."

The room somehow became even quieter.

Ethan sat down.

The basketball player looked deeply offended by this information.

Mr. Bennett arrived three minutes later carrying coffee and disappointment.

"Good morning, emotionally unstable people."

"Morning, Mr. Bennett."

"That sounded unconvincing."

He dropped his notes onto the desk.

The lesson began.

For once Ethan thought maybe everyone would move on.

Maybe.

Then every five minutes somebody looked back at him.

Or Chloe.

Or both.

Like they were trying to solve a conspiracy.

It was exhausting.

Chloe seemed to find it hilarious.

Halfway through class Mr. Bennett wrote a particularly ugly question across the board.

Several students immediately gave up.

One actually sighed dramatically.

"Come on," Bennett said.

"It's not that hard."

Nobody moved.

Ethan glanced at the board.

Found the answer almost immediately.

Years of sitting quietly and actually listening during class occasionally paid off.

Before he could say anything—

Chloe leaned slightly toward him.

"So what's the answer?"

He looked at her.

"You serious?"

"Very."

He explained it quietly.

Thirty seconds.

Maybe less.

Chloe listened.

Nodded once.

Then stood up.

The entire class turned.

Including Mr. Bennett.

"Chloe?"

She answered.

Correctly.

Not partly correct.

Not lucky.

Completely correct.

The room froze.

Mr. Bennett blinked.

Actually blinked.

"...Well."

He looked genuinely impressed.

"That is exactly right."

Several students turned around immediately.

One whispered:

"Since when?"

Another whispered:

"What happened yesterday?"

The basketball player looked personally betrayed.

Chloe sat back down.

Calm.

Like nothing unusual had happened.

Ethan shook his head slightly.

"You could've just let me answer."

She smiled.

"Where's the fun in that?"

The bell rang.

Students poured into the hallway.

Mr. Bennett was still staring suspiciously at Chloe as if he'd witnessed academic fraud.

As Ethan packed his bag, Chloe nudged his shoulder lightly.

"Thanks."

"It's nothing."

"No."

She zipped up her bag.

"That would've taken me ten minutes."

"It took you thirty seconds."

"Because you explained it."

Ethan shrugged.

She looked at him for a second.

Thinking.

Then:

"I'm buying you lunch."

"What?"

"Lunch."

"You don't owe me lunch."

"I do."

"You absolutely don't."

"I already decided."

"That's concerning."

"Good."

And before he could argue further—

She walked away.

Leaving him standing there.

Again.

12:17 PM — Cafeteria

This was a terrible idea.

Ethan knew it immediately.

Because Chloe's table was impossible to miss.

Not because they were loud.

Not because they demanded attention.

Because attention arrived automatically.

Five girls.

All attractive.

All popular.

All people Ethan usually observed from approximately three social universes away.

And now—

He was walking toward them.

Several nearby tables noticed instantly.

A guy actually stopped eating.

Just stopped.

Fork halfway to his mouth.

Watching.

Wonderful.

Chloe waved him over.

"Over here."

Too late to escape now.

He sat down.

The table looked at him.

Not hostile.

Just curious.

Very curious.

There were four girls sitting with Chloe.

Claire's seat was empty.

A girl with dark brown hair and sharp green eyes pointed at him immediately.

"OH."

"There he is."

Ethan sighed.

"There who is?"

"The mystery boy."

"I hate that title already."

The girls laughed.

Chloe rolled her eyes.

"Ethan, this is Ava."

Ava grinned.

"The famous Ethan."

"I'm not famous."

"You got reposted by Chloe Hopper."

"That's not how fame works."

"It absolutely is at this school."

The girl beside Ava laughed.

She had curly black hair and a calm expression.

"Ignore her. She's been waiting all morning to meet you."

"I have not."

"You literally have."

"I was curious."

"That's the same thing."

Ethan was already regretting sitting down.

Chloe looked far too entertained.

Ava pointed between them.

"Okay, explain."

"No."

"Chloe."

"No."

"Ethan."

"Definitely no."

Ava groaned dramatically.

"You people are impossible."

"Thank you," Ethan said.

"That wasn't a compliment."

"It is now."

That earned another round of laughter.

For the next few minutes conversation drifted naturally.

Movies.

Teachers.

A disastrous chemistry experiment.

Someone's terrible ex-boyfriend.

Ava somehow managed to tell three different stories without taking a breath.

Ethan mostly listened.

Which, surprisingly, seemed to make everyone more comfortable.

Because nobody had to compete for attention.

They could just talk.

At one point Ava mentioned Claire.

"She's probably asleep right now."

"She texted me during third period," Chloe said.

"Still sick?"

"Yeah."

Ava nodded.

"Good. She deserves it."

The table stared at her.

"What?"

"You don't deserve being sick."

"Oh."

Ava pointed her fork.

"That's what I meant."

Chloe laughed.

For a moment Ethan found himself watching her.

Head tilted slightly.

Completely relaxed.

Different from the Chloe everyone else saw.

And Ethan suddenly understood something.

People always assumed Chloe loved attention.

But sitting here—

Watching closely—

It wasn't attention she liked.

It was people.

There was a difference.

A big one.

Eventually Ava looked at Ethan.

"You're way different than I expected."

He sighed.

"You too?"

"What does that mean?"

"Everyone keeps saying that."

Chloe smirked.

"Maybe that's because nobody paid attention before."

The table went quiet for half a second.

Not awkward.

Just true.

Ethan looked down at his food.

Didn't really know what to do with that.

Across from him Chloe smiled slightly.

The real smile.

The one from Mabel's.

The one from the clinic.

Not the hallway version.

And for some reason—

That one always felt different.

Around them the cafeteria remained loud.

Hundreds of students.

Endless conversations.

But for the first time in a very long time—

Ethan Parker wasn't sitting alone.

And apparently half the school couldn't stop talking about it.
