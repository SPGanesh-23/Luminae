The Cake Shop on the Third Block

6:58 PM — Westwood Community Clinic

It was Diana's idea.

Ethan had his bag over his shoulder and was halfway to the door when she said it — not loudly, just with the calm certainty of someone who had been horizontal for six hours and was still somehow winning every argument in the room.

"It's late," she said. "And dark. And still raining."

She looked at Chloe.

"Ethan can walk you home."

"Mom. I walk home alone all the time."

"You walk home at three in the afternoon. This is different."

They both looked at Ethan.

Ethan, who had absolutely no idea which direction Chloe lived, said, "Yeah. Same direction."

Chloe gave him a look that said she knew that was a lie.

He gave her one back that said — your mother just collapsed, let her have this.

Something in Chloe's expression shifted.

"Fine."

She leaned over and kissed her mom on the forehead.

"I'll be back tomorrow morning."

"I'll be discharged by then."

"I'll be back anyway."

Diana smiled — tired and real. She looked at Ethan one last time.

"Thank you. For today. All of it."

"Get some sleep."

Ethan said softly

She closed her eyes before they'd even reached the door.

7:04 PM — the street outside

The rain had softened to a mist — the kind that didn't fall so much as simply exist in the air, settling on everything quietly.

"Which way?"

Ethan asked.

"Left. Then straight."

They started walking.

For the first block, neither of them said anything.

The silence wasn't hostile. It was just the natural result of two people who had shared something unexpectedly real in a small room and now had too much open air around them.

Ethan looked at the pavement. Chloe looked at her phone once, then put it in her pocket.

Second block.

Still nothing.

"You didn't have to say you were going the same way."

"I know."

"So why did you?"

"Because your mom asked. Seemed like the least I could do."

Chloe went quiet.

They walked on.

Third block

Chloe slowed.

She checked her watch — 7:19 PM — and then looked across the street. A small shop, warm light in the window, hand-painted letters on the glass: Mabel's.

She stopped walking entirely.

"It's not too late,"

she said. Almost to herself.

She glanced at him.

"There's a cake shop. That one. It's actually a really great spot but I haven't been in over two months. I just—"

She stopped. Like she was talking herself into something.

"I think this is probably the right time. If you don't mind."

Ethan looked at the shop. Then back at her.

She hadn't really eaten anything since she'd arrived at the clinic. He'd noticed.

"Yeah. Let's go."

Chloe blinked like she'd expected more convincing. Then she crossed the street without another word and he followed.

Inside, the shop was warm and smelled like butter and vanilla and something faintly caramelized. The kind of smell that made you feel like you'd been somewhere safe a long time ago.

Late enough that every table was empty. No customers, no noise — just the soft clink of something in the kitchen and the old woman behind the counter who looked up when the door opened.

Small and white-haired, apron dusted with flour. She looked like she'd been baking since before either of them existed.

Then she saw Chloe.

"Oh — my goodness. Chloe, is that you?"

Chloe's face did something Ethan had never seen it do at school.

It lit up.

"Grandma Mabel!"

She went straight to the counter and leaned over it and Mabel held both her hands across the glass and looked at her the way you look at something you've been quietly missing.

"Two months! Two whole months and not a word. I thought you'd moved away."

"I know, I know, I'm sorry. Things got busy and then I kept meaning to come and then—"

"And then you almost didn't."

"And then I almost didn't."

Mabel shook her head slowly but she was smiling.

"You look tired, my love."

"My mom's at the clinic. She collapsed today. She's okay now but—"

"Oh, that poor woman. She works too hard."

"She really does."

Mabel squeezed her hands once and then looked past Chloe at Ethan standing near the door.

"And who's this?"

Chloe turned like she'd briefly forgotten he existed.

"This is Ethan. His mom is the nurse looking after my mom at the clinic. He — walked me here."

"Hi,"

Ethan said.

"Hello, Ethan. Come in, don't stand at the door like a delivery man."

He came in.

Mabel led them to the display case — tiered shelves of cakes and tarts and small decorated things that looked like they'd been made with the kind of patience that only existed before everyone had a phone.

Chloe leaned over the glass the way she probably had since she was small, scanning slowly, thinking.

Before she could say anything, Mabel appeared from behind the counter carrying a small plate.

On it sat a slice of something pale golden with a thin glaze and a small round tart beside it.

"I remembered,"

Mabel said simply.

Chloe stared at the plate.

"You still make the honey almond cake?"

"Every Tuesday. And today is Tuesday, so here you are."

Chloe looked up at her and for a second she seemed very close to saying something she couldn't find the words for. Instead she just said:

"I missed this."

"I know you did."

Mabel set the plate on the nearest table and turned to Ethan with the pleasant efficiency of someone who had been feeding people their whole life.

"And what would you like, dear?"

She looked between them. Then, with the serenity of someone who had absolutely made up her mind about something:

"Or should I just ask — what does your boyfriend want?"

The word landed in the middle of the room like a dropped tray.

Ethan went very still.

Chloe turned pink in a way that she absolutely did not intend and absolutely could not stop.

"He's — no. He's not my boyfriend. He's my friend. We just — he just walked me here."

Mabel looked between them with the expression of someone who had watched a great many young people say exactly that and had never once believed it.

"Of course, of course. So. What would your friend like?"

Ethan looked at the case. Looked at Chloe's plate — the honey almond cake, the lemon tart. Then back at Mabel.

"Whatever she's having. Looks good."

Mabel nodded like he'd passed some test and disappeared into the kitchen.

Chloe sat down and looked at her plate and very deliberately did not look at Ethan.

"Your face is red,"

he said.

A pause.

"It's warm in here."

"It's not that warm."

She finally looked at him. He wasn't smiling exactly — but there was something in the corners of his expression that was close.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

He sat down across from her. Mabel came back with his plate and set it down quietly, then retreated to the kitchen with the tact of someone who knew when to leave people alone.

Just the two of them, in a warm empty cake shop, at half past seven on a rainy Tuesday.

Inside Mabel's

They ate for a minute without talking.

Chloe took a bite of the honey almond cake and her eyes closed for a second.

"I forgot how good this was."

"When did you start coming here?"

Ethan asked.

"My dad used to bring me. He called it our Tuesday ritual even when it wasn't Tuesday."

She smiled at the plate.

"Mabel always remembered what I liked. Even after—"

She didn't finish the sentence. She didn't need to.

"He sounds like he was a good dad."

"The best kind of loud. The kind that made everything feel okay even when it probably wasn't."

"What about yours?" she asked. "Your dad."

A beat.

"He's abroad. Working. There were loans from before I was born — family debts. He went to pay them off."

He said it simply, like a fact he'd long since made peace with.

"How long has he been gone?"

"Seven years."

Chloe looked at him properly.

"Seven years is a long time."

"Yeah."

"Do you talk to him?"

"Sometimes. The time difference makes it hard."

He turned the fork over in his hand.

"I think he genuinely believes he'll come back when the debt is cleared and everything will be fine. But time doesn't pause while you're gone."

Chloe was quiet for a moment.

"No," she said softly. "It really doesn't."

They sat with that for a second — two people who had both learned early that absence came in different shapes and that none of them were easy.

"You're different, you know."

Ethan looked up.

"From other guys at school. The ones who talk to me usually have a whole thing going on. You can feel it. Like there's a strategy."

She traced a line on the table with one finger.

"You don't do that. You just say things. Like they're actually true."

"They are actually true."

"That's what I mean."

Ethan didn't say anything to that.

But something in him settled quietly, like a word he'd been looking for and finally found.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Yeah."

"Do you ever get tired of being invisible?"

The question surprised him. Not because it was rude — but because it was precise.

"Sometimes. But I think I got so used to watching from the edges that being in the middle of things feels—"

"Loud?"

"Loud. And kind of exhausting."

Chloe looked at him with something close to recognition.

"I know that feeling."

"I know you do."

She didn't ask how he knew.

She just nodded, slowly, like something had been confirmed.

8:40 PM — time to leave

Chloe looked at the empty plates and then at the clock above the counter.

"We should go."

She stood and walked toward the kitchen doorway.

"Grandma Mabel? We're heading out. Thank you — really—"

She stopped.

Ethan hadn't moved toward the door.

He was looking at the stack of dishes on the counter near the sink — the day's worth of plates and tins and trays that hadn't been touched yet.

"Hey — I think we still have some time. Can we help you clean up before we go?"

Chloe turned and stared at him.

Mabel appeared from the back drying her hands, and looked at Ethan the way you look at someone who has just done something unexpectedly right.

"Oh, you really don't have to—"

"I know. But it's late and there are a lot of dishes."

A beat.

"Well. I won't say no to that."

She handed Ethan an apron that was slightly too short for him and pointed him toward the sink. She handed Chloe a dish towel.

Then she sat down at one of the small tables with a cup of tea and watched them the way grandmothers watch things — with the quiet satisfaction of someone who has seen enough of the world to know when something is right.

They washed and dried in a rhythm that took a few minutes to find and then became easy. Ethan passed. Chloe caught. Mabel occasionally called out where things went.

It was — unexpectedly — one of the more comfortable silences Chloe had been in for a long time.

"You're weirdly good at dishes."

"Empty apartment, remember. You learn."

She smiled at that. The real one.

At some point Mabel came over to put away the last of the cake tins, and as she passed Chloe she leaned in close and said quietly, right next to her ear:

"You two make a good pair."

Chloe felt heat rise to her face immediately.

"Grandma Mabel — no. He's my friend. I just met him today."

Mabel set the tin on the shelf and turned back, completely unbothered.

"So did I. And I like him very much."

Ethan, at the sink, said nothing.

But the tips of his ears had gone faintly red.

Chloe noticed.

She said nothing either.

By nine o'clock the kitchen was clean and Mabel was at the door seeing them out.

"Come back sooner this time."

"I will. I promise."

She hugged her — small and warm and the kind that lasted a few seconds longer than a polite one.

"Take care of yourself, my love. And your mother."

Mabel looked at Ethan.

"You too, young man. The honey almond is yours whenever you want it."

"Thank you. For having us."

She waved them off like she'd known them both for years, and shut the door.

9:07 PM — the last few blocks

The mist had thickened while they were inside. The streetlights wore soft amber halos.

They walked side by side — not the careful distance of two strangers, but something that had found its own comfortable width without either of them deciding it.

"That was your idea," Chloe said. "Staying to help."

"She had a lot of dishes."

"You didn't have to."

"I know."

She walked in silence for a moment.

"It was good. Thank you."

He nodded.

"She really liked you," Chloe said. "Mabel. She doesn't warm up to people quickly. It took her three visits before she remembered my order."

"She remembered yours after two months."

"That's different. That's love."

She said it lightly but meant it.

They reached her street. The house with the light in the upstairs window, the pot by the front door that had given up flowering for the season.

They stopped at the gate.

Chloe turned to face him. In the amber light she looked less like the person who commanded classrooms and more like someone who'd had a long honest day and was quietly glad it had ended the way it had.

"You're not what I expected."

"You didn't expect anything. You didn't know I existed."

She made a face — acknowledging a fair point she'd rather not acknowledge.

"You're not what I would have expected if I had expected something."

"That's better."

A small silence. Comfortable.

Then she pulled out her phone.

"Your Instagram. What is it?"

Ethan paused for just a half second — and then turned his phone toward her. His personal account. Not AlwaysThere4U. Just ethan_p, eleven posts, mostly rain through windows and the occasional book spine.

She followed him. He followed back.

"Okay,"

she said.

"Okay."

She pushed open the gate. Paused with her hand on it.

"Thank you. For the clinic and the walk and the cake shop and the dishes."

He almost smiled.

"It was a good Tuesday."

She looked at him — the look he was starting to recognize as the one she used when she actually meant what came next.

"Yeah. It really was."

She turned to go inside.

"Hey — take care. Text me anytime you want. Even if it's nothing."

Chloe paused at the door with her key in the lock.

"Even if it's nothing,"

she said.

Not a question. Just her turning the words over quietly.

She went inside.

11:58 PM — Ethan's room

He got home to the empty apartment and the quiet and the 200-something unread messages waiting in AlwaysThere4U.

He answered them slowly. The way he always did.

Short replies to people falling apart at midnight. Careful replies to people pretending they weren't. A few jokes where jokes were needed. A few honest sentences where they weren't.

Outside his window, rain tapped softly against the glass.

At 12:17 AM his personal phone buzzed.

Ethan stared at the message for a second before answering.

yeah. always.

Three dots appeared almost immediately.

His fingers hovered over the keyboard longer than they should have.

panicking usually makes things worse.

Then:

Ethan blinked.

The first emoji she'd used all conversation.

Ethan leaned back in his chair.

There were a hundred ways to answer that. Most of them true.

The typing bubble appeared immediately this time.

Ethan stared at the words.

Something strange tightened quietly in his chest.

He didn't know what to do with compliments when they sounded honest.

She sent another laughing emoji.

Then:

Chloe sent three laughing emojis in a row this time.

He could practically hear it.

The real version. Not the polished hallway one.

The conversation slowed after that.

Not awkward. Just tired.

Comfortable tired. chloeh_ : hey ethan? ethan_p : yeah? chloeh_ : i'm glad it was you in that room today.

He looked at that message for a long moment.

Then finally typed:

get some sleep, Chloe.
goodnight.

A few seconds later:

goodnight ethan :)

He set his phone down carefully beside him.

On his other screen, the AlwaysThere4U notifications were still blinking endlessly.

New confessions. New breakdowns. New strangers trying not to feel alone at midnight.

And somewhere in them, just.a.nobody00 was still offline — still unknown, still the other conversation waiting quietly in the dark.

Ethan looked between both screens for a moment.

Then he closed the laptop and kept his phone close instead.

Some things could wait until tomorrow.

Tonight had been enough.
