✦ Chapter 7 ✦

Lines You Don’t Cross

✦

The hallway outside the principal’s office smelled faintly of paper and old wood polish.

Alex sat alone on the bench near the door, lazily spinning a pen between his fingers while checking the time on his watch for what felt like the hundredth time.

Inside the office, Ethan was getting another lecture.

Raised voices occasionally slipped through the door.

“…your actions reflect on the entire school…”

“…violence will not be tolerated…”

“…under my supervision…”

Alex leaned his head back against the wall and sighed.

Some things never changed.

Ethan getting dragged into the principal’s office was practically a weekly event at this point.

The only difference now was that the principal actually had enough authority to make even Ethan listen.

At least a little.

Alex checked the time again.

Five minutes.

Ten.

Still not out.

“You look bored.”

Alex glanced up instantly.

Lara stood a few feet away, one hand lightly gripping the strap of her bag.

For a second, he genuinely looked confused.

Then his eyes dropped to the black Riverview Academy ID card hanging around her neck.

He blinked.

“…What?”

Lara raised an eyebrow.

“That’s a very warm welcome.”

“No, wait—”

Alex sat up properly.

“You study here now?”

“Apparently.”

“Since when?”

“Since this morning.”

He stared at her another second like his brain still hadn’t caught up.

“You joined today?”

“Yeah.”

“And nobody told me?”

“I didn’t tell anyone,” she admitted softly.

Alex frowned slightly.

“Why?”

Her fingers brushed against the edge of the ID card absentmindedly.

“Dad’s been busy ever since he became principal,” she said quietly. “I thought maybe if I joined here…” she hesitated briefly, “…I could spend more time with him.”

The sentence was simple.

Quiet.

But Alex understood immediately.

Ever since the principal arrived early, things in Lara’s life had changed too.

More meetings.

Longer hours.

Less time at home.

Small consequences.

The kind nobody notices at first.

Alex looked away briefly before muttering,

“You could’ve at least warned me.”

A faint smile appeared on Lara’s face.

“And ruin your reaction?”

Alex rolled his eyes slightly.

“That bad?”

“You looked traumatized.”

“I was.”

She laughed softly.

And somehow, the tension in the hallway eased.

They talked quietly while waiting outside the office.

Mostly random things.

Teachers.

Class timings.

How terrible the school coffee was.

Lara trusted him strangely fast.

Maybe because of the library night.

Maybe because Alex never tried too hard around her.

There was something calm about him when he wasn’t fighting someone.

Then—

A crash echoed faintly from down the corridor.

Both of them paused.

Another sound followed.

A girl’s voice.

“Stop—!”

Alex’s expression changed instantly.

Not dramatically.

That was the terrifying part.

The softness simply vanished from his face.

His eyes shifted toward the storage room near the staircase.

Lara noticed it immediately.

“Alex…?”

But he was already walking.

Fast.

The storage room door was slightly open.

Alex pushed it wider.

Inside—

Two senior boys stood cornering a girl near the shelves.

One of them had her wrist tightly grabbed while the other laughed at something she clearly wanted no part in.

The moment Alex saw it—

something inside him snapped.

“What the hell are you doing?” one of the boys asked sharply.

Alex didn’t answer.

He moved first.

The punch landed so hard the first boy slammed sideways into the metal shelf beside him.

Boxes crashed violently onto the floor.

The sound exploded through the cramped room.

The second boy cursed and rushed toward him—

Alex grabbed him by the collar and drove him straight into the wall.

The entire shelf rattled hard enough to shake loose stacks of old files.

Lara froze near the doorway.

This wasn’t like the cafeteria incident.

This wasn’t controlled.

Alex looked furious in a way that didn’t even seem human anymore.

The first boy tried getting back up.

Alex kicked him back down immediately.

No hesitation.

No warning.

The second student swung wildly in panic, but Alex barely reacted.

He blocked the hit and slammed his fist directly across the boy’s face hard enough to spray blood onto the floor.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Each impact echoed violently through the storage room.

The second boy’s voice finally cracked into genuine fear.

“STOP—!”

Boxes kept falling from the shelves every time Alex shoved him backward.

The terrified girl finally managed to pull herself free and hurried toward the exit.

But Alex didn’t stop.

The second boy scrambled backward desperately across the floor, knocking over loose papers and broken boxes as he tried to escape.

“You’re fucking insane—!”

Alex grabbed him by the collar again and slammed him against the shelf hard enough to bend the metal slightly inward.

Something burned violently inside him now.

A single thought pushed through the rage.

Bare hands didn’t feel enough anymore.

The thought lasted less than a second.

Instinctive.

Raw.

And silently—

the Reality Entity listened.

Not emotionally.

Not morally.

Only logically.

Outside the hallway, another set of footsteps rapidly approached the principal’s office.

A student athlete carrying a sports bag slowed after hearing Lara scream earlier.

Confused, he instinctively followed Ethan and the principal toward the storage room.

The zipper on the bag had been left partly open after practice.

An aluminum baseball bat rested loosely inside.

Nothing unusual.

Nothing impossible.

Just timing.

Convenient timing.

“Alex STOP!”

Lara’s voice echoed through the room.

Nothing.

It was like he couldn’t even hear her.

Fear crawled up Lara’s spine.

She stumbled backward before turning and running toward the principal’s office.

“ETHAN!”

The office door swung open instantly.

Ethan stepped out first, irritation already visible on his face—

until he saw Lara’s expression.

His face changed immediately.

“What happened?”

“Storage room— Alex—”

Ethan didn’t wait for the rest.

He ran.

The principal followed behind him.

The sports student followed instinctively behind them.

By the time they reached the room, one of the boys was curled beside the shelf coughing violently while the other struggled weakly beneath Alex’s grip.

The room itself barely looked recognizable anymore.

Broken boxes littered the floor.

Loose papers drifted everywhere.

One of the shelves leaned dangerously sideways from the repeated impacts.

Alex grabbed the student by the shirt and punched him again.

The boy cried out weakly this time.

Not angry anymore.

Terrified.

“Alex!”

Ethan caught his arm forcefully.

Alex shoved him away without even looking.

Ethan stumbled back a step in surprise.

The principal stopped cold near the doorway.

And for the first time since arriving at Riverview—

he looked genuinely afraid of a student.

“Alex,” Ethan said again, firmer this time.

No response.

It genuinely looked like Alex couldn’t hear anything around him anymore.

Then—

As the sports student hurried into the room behind Ethan, the half-open bag hanging from his shoulder slipped.

The aluminum baseball bat slid free.

CLANG.

The metallic sound echoed sharply across the storage room floor.

Everyone’s eyes shifted instinctively.

Even Alex’s.

For half a second—

silence.

The Reality Entity observed.

Probability aligned.

Alex slowly let go of the student’s collar.

Then bent down and picked up the bat.

Lara’s face lost color instantly.

“Alex…”

The terrified student on the floor tried scrambling backward.

His hands shook so badly he could barely move.

Alex stared at him silently.

And somehow—

that silence felt worse than the violence.

The rage inside him wanted damage now.

Real damage.

The bat swung.

CRACK.

The sound echoed violently through the room.

The boy screamed.

“ENOUGH!”

Ethan lunged forward immediately.

Alex raised the bat again—

but Ethan caught it mid-swing with both hands.

The force alone pushed him backward slightly.

Even Ethan struggled to hold him there.

Alex’s expression didn’t even look angry anymore.

That was the frightening part.

It looked empty.

Focused.

Like violence had become the only thing left inside him.

“Alex!” Ethan shouted.

Still nothing.

The principal stood frozen near the doorway in horror.

The sports student stared at his own bat in Alex’s hands like he couldn’t process what was happening.

Then—

“Alex!”

Lara’s voice cut through the room.

Silence.

Alex stopped instantly.

His grip loosened slightly.

His breathing remained rough.

Uneven.

Slowly, he turned his head toward the doorway.

Toward her.

And little by little…

the rage faded from his eyes.

Not completely.

But enough.

Enough for Ethan to finally rip the bat from his hands.

The metal weapon clattered loudly across the floor.

Nobody moved.

Nobody spoke.

The room was a disaster.

Broken boxes scattered everywhere.

Blood near the shelves.

Bent metal.

Terrified silence hanging in the air.

Alex stared at the two boys one last time with pure disgust before pulling his arm free from Ethan’s grip.

Then he walked out.

Without a single word.

Lara stood frozen near the doorway.

The principal still looked shaken.

Like he had just witnessed something he couldn’t fully process.

Ethan exhaled slowly while rubbing the back of his neck.

“…I’ll handle this,” he muttered.

The principal looked at him sharply.

“Handle this? He could’ve seriously injured them!”

Ethan didn’t argue.

Because honestly—

he wasn’t wrong.

After a few moments, the principal left to call medical staff and security.

That left Ethan and Lara standing alone near the destroyed storage room.

Lara looked down the hallway Alex disappeared into.

Then quietly asked,

“…Does he always do that?”

Ethan stayed silent for a moment.

Then he leaned against the wall beside the doorway.

“No,” he said honestly. “Usually he’s calm.”

His eyes shifted toward the frightened girl sitting near the shelves.

“But Alex has one line he absolutely cannot tolerate.”

Lara frowned slightly.

Ethan folded his arms.

“You can insult him.”

“You can hit him.”

“You can spread rumors about him.”

His expression darkened slightly.

“But the second he sees a girl getting harassed…”

He glanced at the blood on the floor.

“…he loses control.”

Lara stayed quiet.

“That’s why I usually stay close to him during fights,” Ethan continued. “Most of the time, I’m the only person who can stop him.”

Then he looked at her properly.

“But today was different.”

Lara blinked.

“When you shouted his name…” Ethan said slowly, “…he calmed down.”

A thoughtful crease formed between his brows.

“That almost never happens.”

Lara looked down the hallway again.

Toward the direction Alex had walked away.

The image of the quiet boy who walked her home beneath streetlights…

and the boy holding a bloodstained baseball bat…

didn’t feel like the same person.

And for the first time since meeting him—

she realized there were parts of Alex she still didn’t understand at all.

Somewhere behind the lenses resting quietly on Alex’s face—

the Reality Entity continued learning.

Not how to create.

Not how to destroy.

But how easily human desire could reshape reality itself.
