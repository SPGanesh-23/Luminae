✦ Chapter 13 ✦

What Makes Him Stand

✦

The hallway beyond the east wing remained silent.

Too silent.

The seniors stood blocking the path toward the old library building, evening light stretching across the floor beneath their feet.

Alex and Lara stopped several meters away.

Nobody moved at first.

The injured boy with the bandaged nose pointed toward Alex again.

“That’s him.”

Alex’s expression remained unreadable.

One of the taller seniors near the center folded his arms slowly while studying him.

Honestly—

Alex looked smaller than they expected.

Quiet.

Thin.

Almost unimpressive.

If they hadn’t seen the injuries themselves, they probably wouldn’t have believed the stories.

“You really did all that?” another senior asked casually.

The bruised student beside him spoke immediately.

“He used a bat.”

“And you still got folded?” one of them muttered.

A few quiet laughs followed.

The injured boy looked furious instantly.

Alex stayed calm.

“Move.”

The tallest senior tilted his head slightly.

“Or what?”

Silence.

Lara could feel the tension tightening around the hallway inch by inch.

The seniors weren’t scared.

Not yet.

That alone made this different from the storage room.

The tall senior glanced briefly toward Lara beside him.

Then back toward Alex.

“You know,” he said casually, “people are saying you lost your mind back there.”

Alex didn’t answer.

“You put two students in medical over one argument.”

“One argument?” Alex repeated quietly.

Something about his voice made the hallway colder.

The injured student immediately stepped forward again.

“Don’t act innocent now—”

Alex moved first.

Fast.

His fist crashed directly into the boy’s face hard enough to slam him backward into the wall.

Lara flinched instinctively.

Everything exploded at once.

“HEY—!”

Another senior grabbed Alex from behind.

Alex twisted sharply and drove his elbow backward into the student’s ribs before shoving him aside.

But this time—

there were too many.

A punch slammed across Alex’s jaw from the side.

Another hit landed against his stomach.

Someone shoved him hard into the lockers lining the hallway.

Metal rattled violently.

Lara’s heartbeat spiked instantly.

“STOP!”

Nobody listened.

Alex swung back hard, landing another clean hit across someone’s cheek—

but another student immediately grabbed his shoulder while a different punch crashed into his ribs.

This wasn’t like the store room incident.

There was no uncontrollable rage.

No terrifying silence.

Just a brutal unfair fight against too many people at once.

And slowly—

Alex started losing ground.

A hit split the corner of his lip.

Another slammed into his stomach hard enough to force the air from his lungs.

The tall senior grabbed his collar and shoved him violently against the lockers.

“You think you’re some psycho hero now?”

Alex shoved him away immediately—

but a fist crashed into the side of his face before he could recover.

His glasses slipped loose.

Then fell.

CLACK.

The thin black frame skidded across the hallway floor.

For half a second—

Alex froze.

A sharp ringing hit his ears instantly.

The world blurred slightly.

Lara saw the glasses slide near her feet.

Without thinking, she quickly bent down and picked them up.

The moment her fingers touched the frame—

something flickered.

A faint distortion rippled silently across the lenses.

Like purple static trapped beneath glass.

Lara blinked sharply.

For the smallest moment—

the hallway around her glitched.

Not visibly.

Not fully.

Just enough to feel wrong.

Like reality itself skipped a breath.

Then it vanished.

“Lara!”

She looked up immediately.

Alex had been shoved hard onto the floor.

One of the seniors kicked him sharply across the side.

Pain shot violently through his ribs.

Another grabbed the front of his shirt.

“Not so scary now, huh?”

Alex swung back instantly and punched him across the mouth hard enough to loosen the grip—

but another blow immediately slammed into the back of his shoulder.

The hallway echoed with impacts.

Lara gripped the glasses tighter unconsciously and wished that her dad could come to her magically.

Then—

One of the seniors stepped away from the fight.

Not one of the injured boys.

Someone else.

Calmer.

He glanced toward Lara standing near the wall.

Then walked toward her slowly.

“Well,” he muttered casually, fixing his sleeve, “this got messy.”

Lara instinctively stepped back slightly.

He noticed immediately.

“Relax,” he said. “Nobody’s gonna hurt you.”

His eyes flicked briefly toward the glasses in her hand.

Behind him—

Alex barely pushed himself halfway up before another punch forced him back down again.

The senior near Lara sighed softly.

“You should probably stay away from him,” he said casually. “Guy’s clearly insane.”

Then—

without warning—

he reached toward her wrist.

Not violently.

Just casually.

Like he expected no resistance.

But the second his fingers touched her—

something inside Alex snapped.

Not rage.

Not insanity.

Something sharper.

Focused.

Protective.

Alex’s head lifted instantly.

And for the first time since the fight started—

his eyes changed.

The exhaustion disappeared.

The pain disappeared.

Only focus remained.

“Don’t touch her.”

The hallway went strangely quiet.

Even the senior near Lara paused slightly.

Alex slowly stood up.

Blood ran faintly from the corner of his mouth.

His breathing was rough now.

Uneven.

But steady.

One of the students laughed nervously.

“Bro can barely stand.”

Alex wiped blood from his lip with the back of his hand.

Then walked forward.

The first senior rushed him immediately—

Alex ducked the swing and drove his fist directly into the student’s stomach before slamming his elbow across the side of his face.

The student collapsed instantly.

Another tried grabbing him from behind.

Alex twisted violently and smashed his head backward into the student’s nose.

A crack echoed sharply.

The injured boys backed away immediately.

Fear returning.

Not because Alex looked insane now.

Because somehow—

he looked completely in control.

Every movement became sharper.

Cleaner.

More deliberate.

Another senior swung wildly.

Alex caught the wrist mid-motion and slammed him directly into the lockers hard enough to shake the entire row.

The hallway erupted into panic again.

“WHAT THE HELL—”

“GET HIM—”

But nobody wanted to move first anymore.

The senior near Lara slowly stepped backward now.

His grip gone from her wrist.

Alex immediately moved between him and Lara without even looking back.

That tiny action—

that instinctive positioning—

made Lara’s chest tighten strangely.

The tall senior cursed under his breath before rushing Alex again with two others.

This time the fight became chaos.

Punches.

Shouting.

Bodies slamming into metal lockers.

Alex took hits too.

Hard ones.

But he kept moving forward anyway.

Then—

Bright headlights suddenly flashed through the hallway windows from outside.

Everyone froze instinctively.

A car door slammed.

Footsteps approached rapidly from the entrance.

The principal rounded the corner—

and stopped dead.

Silence.

His eyes immediately took in everything at once.

The blood.

The damaged lockers.

The terrified students.

Alex standing protectively in front of Lara.

And Lara herself—

still holding Alex’s glasses tightly in her hand.

The seniors panicked instantly.

“Shit.”

“GO.”

Within seconds they scattered down the opposite hallway.

Even the injured boys ran.

The principal shouted after them furiously—

but they were already disappearing around the corner.

Then silence returned.

Heavy breathing filled the hallway.

Alex finally staggered slightly.

The adrenaline was fading now.

Pain hit all at once.

Lara immediately moved toward him.

“You idiot,” she whispered shakily.

Alex let out a weak breath that almost sounded like a laugh.

“…they started it.”

“You’re bleeding.”

“…probably.”

The principal stared between both of them, still trying to process what he had walked into.

Then Lara slowly looked down at the glasses still resting in her hands.

The lenses reflected the hallway lights strangely.

For half a second—

she thought she saw something moving inside them.

A faint violet distortion.

Watching.

Then the reflection returned to normal.

Slowly—

she handed the glasses back to Alex.

The moment he put them on—

somewhere far beyond reality—

fractured screens flickered once again.

— End of Chapter 13 —
