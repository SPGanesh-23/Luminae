✦ Chapter 5✦

The Survivors

✦

Kyros woke to darkness.

Cold concrete pressed against his spine. The air smelled of dust and metal. His head throbbed in slow pulses—each one sharper than the last.

He tried to move.

Pain exploded behind his eyes.

He sucked in a breath and forced himself upright. Shapes emerged through the dim light. Reinforced walls. Steel doors bolted shut. Pipes snaking along the ceiling. The low hum of a generator.

Underground.

A voice drifted through the dark.

“Finally awake.”

Kyros’ gaze snapped toward it.

A girl stepped forward from the shadows. Short hair. Steady eyes. Not hostile—but not relaxed either. She stood balanced, ready to move if he did.

Recognition hit him.

“You…” His voice was rough. “You hit me.”

“I did,” she said calmly. No apology. No pride either. Just fact. “You were about to walk into a kill zone. And my crew doesn’t take risks lightly.”

Crew.

His memories slammed together—the fight in the square. The man with the watch. The surge. Then—

A blow from behind.

Her.

Kyros steadied himself against the wall. “You could’ve killed me.”

She tilted her head slightly. “If I wanted you dead, you wouldn’t have woken up.”

A pause.

Then she gestured toward a steel door. “Come. You deserve answers.”

The door slid open with a metallic scrape.

The basement widened into something far larger than he expected.

Above them, through cracks in the ceiling structure, he could see the decayed remains of an old school building—century-old brick, collapsing beams, shattered windows. Abandoned to rot.

Below it, carved into earth and reinforced with steel, was a bunker.

Lights flickered along support pillars. Worktables cluttered with maps. Crates stacked in corners. A digital clock mounted on the far wall ticking steadily.

Normal time.

For now.

Kyros followed her into the center of the room.

Five figures sat around a metal table. All of them alert. All of them watching him like he was an unexploded device.

And at the far end—

The man he had knocked down in the square.

Bandaged. Bruised. Very much awake.

The scarred man at the head of the table stood first.

“I’m Tarek,” he said, voice controlled, steady. Leadership without effort. “That’s Liora. Kai. Soren. Jax.”

Each of them gave a small nod.

The girl beside Kyros stepped forward. “Iris.”

Kyros absorbed the names.

The injured man exhaled sharply. “Ron,” he muttered. “In case you forgot.”

Kyros met his eyes. “Hard to forget.”

Tension lingered—but it wasn’t chaotic. It was measured.

Iris leaned slightly closer to Kyros.

“Let’s start simple,” she said. “How are you unaffected by the distortion?”

He frowned. “Distortion?”

“The time fractures,” Tarek clarified. “The pauses. The acceleration waves. You moved through them without a watch.”

Kyros looked around the table.

Watches. All of them wore one.

His pulse slowed. “Then tell me what’s happening. The city freezes. Then it moves too fast. Then it’s normal again. It’s like time can’t decide what it wants to be.”

Liora straightened slightly.

“It’s not random,” she said softly. “It’s structured.”

She tapped the digital clock.

“Three seconds. Complete pause. Everything stops. Matter. Motion. Sound.”

Kai continued, picking up the rhythm. “Then three seconds at double speed. Two times acceleration. Gravity shifts slightly. Reflexes distort. Most people can’t react fast enough.”

Jax folded his arms. “Then five minutes of normal time. Stable. Predictable. Until the cycle resets.”

Kyros processed it slowly.

“Three seconds pause,” he murmured. “Three seconds accelerated. Five minutes normal. And it keeps repeating.”

“It’s a loop,” Liora confirmed. “A broken rhythm.”

Soren’s voice was quiet but firm. “Without synchronization, your nervous system misfires during the shift. People seize. Some black out. Some don’t wake up.”

Ron flexed his bandaged hand. “You destroyed my watch. When the surge hit, I nearly got torn apart.”

Kyros’ jaw tightened. “I didn’t know.”

“That’s the problem,” Tarek replied evenly. “You didn’t need one.”

Silence settled over the table.

Jax leaned forward, studying him like a puzzle. “You moved during the pause phase.”

Iris didn’t blink. “And you adjusted during acceleration without lag.”

Kyros looked down at his hands.

“I just… moved,” he said quietly.

Liora’s voice dropped to almost a whisper. “Which means your internal rhythm matches the distortion cycle.”

“Or,” Tarek added carefully, “it’s connected to you.”

That word hung in the air.

Connected.

Kyros felt something tighten in his chest.

“So this isn’t natural,” he said. “This isn’t just some… glitch.”

“No,” Iris answered.

Her eyes locked onto his.

“It started when the entity appeared.”

The room felt colder.

“The distortion is expanding,” Tarek continued. “More unstable each week. The pause windows are sharper. The acceleration waves are stronger.”

Kai gave a thin smile. “We survive because we adapt.”

Jax shrugged. “Barely.”

Kyros looked around at them. At the bunker. At the watches.

“So you brought me here because…”

“Because,” Iris interrupted softly, “you’re the first person we’ve seen who doesn’t need one.”

She stepped closer.

“And that makes you either our solution…”

Ron’s jaw tightened.

“…or the reason this is happening at all.”

Silence.

Heavy.

Kyros flexed his fingers. The faint hum of the bunker filled the gap.

“What now?” he asked.

Tarek’s gaze was unwavering.

“Now,” he said, “we find out what you are.”

And somewhere far above them—

Time paused.

AUTHOR'S NOTE

Before moving into the next chapter, here’s a clear explanation of how the distortion works inside this timeline:

The distortion follows a repeating cycle made up of three phases:

Phase 1 – Pause (3 Seconds)

For exactly three seconds, time completely stops.

Movement freezes.

Sound cuts off.

Objects halt mid-motion.

Even breath and blinking stop.

Only those synchronized with a watch can mentally endure this phase without neurological damage. Most people would collapse or lose consciousness if exposed without protection.

Phase 2 – Acceleration (3 Seconds at 2× Speed)

Immediately after the pause, time surges forward at twice its normal speed for three seconds.

Gravity feels unstable.

Reflexes become unreliable.

Objects move unpredictably fast.

The body struggles to adjust.

Without a calibrated watch, the nervous system cannot process the sudden shift, often resulting in severe disorientation or injury.

Phase 3 – Stabilization (5 Minutes Normal Time)

After the acceleration phase, time returns to normal for five minutes.

Movement stabilizes.

The environment behaves predictably.

Survivors use this window to relocate, repair, and plan.

Then the cycle begins again.

What makes Kyros different is that he moves through all three phases naturally — without wearing a watch.

And that… is not supposed to be possible.

As the story continues, you’ll begin to see that the distortion may not be as fixed as the survivors believe.

— End of Chapter —
