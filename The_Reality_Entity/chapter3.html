Principal’s Daughter

✦

Three Days Later

The house had grown quieter over the past few days.

Not because anyone was fighting.

But because something small had shifted.

Three days after Ethan’s return, the principal stood in the kitchen, adjusting his tie while scanning through emails on his phone. His mind was already at school — pending meetings, teacher evaluations, disciplinary follow-ups.

Across the table, Lara sat with her bag resting at her feet.

“You’ll be late again?” she asked casually.

“Just for a few days,” he replied. “Things are still settling.”

She nodded.

On the refrigerator behind him, two unused museum tickets were still pinned beneath a magnet.

Saturday. 11:30 a.m.

He followed her gaze and cleared his throat.

“We’ll reschedule.”

“It’s okay,” she said softly.

And that was the problem.

It wasn’t okay.

But she wasn’t arguing.

She picked up her bag and headed for the door.

“Text me when you’re done at the library,” he called out.

“I will.”

The door closed gently.

He returned to his emails.

The public library felt warmer than usual that afternoon.

Lara hadn’t meant to stay long. She just didn’t feel like going home yet. The house would be empty until late evening anyway.

She wandered between shelves, pulled out a random novel, and sat near the window.

Across the building, Alex sat alone with an open textbook he hadn’t read in ten minutes.

Ryan had canceled.

Ethan had disappeared somewhere as usual.

So Alex ended up here instead.

He got up to grab another book—

—and nearly walked straight into her.

“Oh—sorry,” he said quickly.

She stepped back. “It’s fine.”

Recognition flickered.

“You’re from Riverview, right?” she asked.

“Yeah.” He paused. “You’re… the principal’s daughter.”

She gave a small nod.

There was a strange pause — not awkward, just unexpected.

They ended up sitting at the same table near the window.

At first, the conversation was light. School. Teachers. Cafeteria jokes.

But underneath it, something else lingered.

“My dad’s been busy,” Lara said after a while, closing her book without marking the page. “New position and everything.”

“Yeah,” Alex replied. “He seems serious.”

“He is.” She smiled faintly. “Sometimes too serious.”

The sky outside had slowly darkened, neither of them noticing.

When Lara finally checked her phone, her eyebrows lifted slightly.

“It’s already this late?”

The library lights had turned brighter. Outside, the street lamps flickered on one by one.

“I should go,” she said, standing.

Alex nodded. “Yeah, same.”

They walked out separately at first.

Outside, the air was cooler. The sky is fully dark now.

Lara stepped a few feet away from the entrance and pulled out her phone.

She called her father.

It rang.

And rang.

And rang.

No answer.

She lowered the phone slowly.

He was probably in a meeting.

Or reviewing files.

Or handling something urgent.

She texted:I’m outside the library.

No response.

She told herself not to overthink it.

She waited.

Five minutes passed.

Then ten.

Cars drove by. The street grew quieter. The library doors closed behind her as the staff locked up for the night.

Twenty minutes.

She tried calling again.

Still nothing.

By the thirty-minute mark, the confidence she’d been holding together began to thin. Not panic — just the uncomfortable feeling of being forgotten.

Not intentionally.

But still.

She hugged her bag a little tighter.

That’s when the library doors opened again.

Alex stepped out, adjusting his backpack strap. He had stayed longer than planned.

He noticed her immediately.

“You’re still here?” he asked.

She tried to sound casual. “Yeah. Just waiting.”

“For your dad?”

She nodded.

He looked at the empty street.

“He’s not picking up?”

She shook her head.

For a second, he hesitated — not because he didn’t care, but because he didn’t want to overstep.

Then he said, “Where do you live?”

“About fifteen minutes from here.”

“I can walk you,” he said simply.

She studied his face.

There was no pity there. No dramatic hero energy.

Just calm certainty.

“You don’t have to,” she said automatically.

“I know.”

That made her pause.

And then, quietly, she nodded.

They walked side by side under the streetlights.

At first, the conversation was light again — small jokes, random comments about school.

But as they turned into quieter residential streets, something shifted.

Lara didn’t feel tense.

She didn’t feel like she had to check behind her every few seconds.

She didn’t feel alone.

Alex wasn’t trying too hard. He wasn’t filling silence with noise. He just walked at her pace.

When a car drove past too quickly, he instinctively moved slightly closer to the roadside.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

She noticed.

“Thanks,” she said softly.

“For what?”

“For… this.”

He shrugged. “It’s not a big deal.”

But to her, it was.

By the time they reached her house, the porch light was still off.

Her father wasn’t home yet.

She felt that small pinch in her chest again.

Alex noticed.

“You good?” he asked.

She nodded. “Yeah.”

But she didn’t step toward the door immediately.

Instead, she looked at him — really looked at him.

Something about the way he handled things. Calm. Present. Steady.

It felt safe.

And that feeling lingered longer than she expected.

“Text me when you’re inside,” he said.

She blinked. “You’re going to wait?”

“Yeah.”

Again — simple.

Certain.

She unlocked the door and stepped inside.

The house was dark.

Quiet.

She closed the door gently behind her… and leaned against it for a second.

Her heart was beating faster than it should have been.

She replayed the walk in her mind — the way he matched her pace, the way he didn’t make it awkward, the way he just stayed.

A faint warmth crept up her cheeks before she could stop it.

She touched her face lightly.

“Why am I blushing?” she muttered under her breath.

Her phone buzzed in her hand.

No — not a message.

Just the screen lighting up as she unlocked it.

She quickly typed:

I’m inside.

Outside, under the streetlight, Alex felt his phone vibrate.

He looked down.

I’m inside.

He exhaled without realizing he’d been holding his breath.

Good, he typed back.Get some rest.

A few seconds later, three dots appeared.

Then:

Thanks again. For walking me.

He stared at the message for a moment longer than necessary.

Anytime, he replied.

Inside the house, Lara read that one word twice.

Anytime.

The faint blush returned, stronger this time.

She locked her phone and stood there in the dim hallway, feeling something new settle quietly in her chest.

Outside, Alex slipped his phone back into his pocket and finally turned to leave.

He didn’t look back.

But he walked home a little lighter than before.

And neither of them realized that something fragile had just begun forming.

Not loud.

Not dramatic.

But real.

A bond built not on coincidence—

—but on presence.

End of Chapter 3

AUTHOR'S NOTE

This bond was never supposed to happen.

If Alex had not wished for Ethan to return, nothing would have shifted.

The new principal would have arrived three days later — as originally scheduled.The suspension files would have remained untouched for a little longer.The museum trip would not have been canceled.

Lara and her father would have spent that Saturday together.

No quiet distance.No unspoken disappointment.No reason for her to stay out longer than usual.

And no reason to be waiting outside the library after dark.

Alex did not directly change emotions.

He did not rewrite personalities.

He simply nudged reality into choosing a slightly different sequence of events.

But small adjustments create new intersections.

The principal came earlier.The museum was canceled.Lara felt the distance.She stayed at the library.Alex happened to be there.

And from that altered sequence, a connection formed — one that was never part of the original path.

Reality was not broken.

It was redirected.

And sometimes, redirection creates bonds that were never meant to exist.

— End of Chapter 3 —
