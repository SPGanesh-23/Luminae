✦ Chapter 9 ✦

Detention

✦

The atmosphere after lunch felt… different.

Not normal.

But calmer.

The tension that had swallowed the classroom during the morning had faded little by little after Lara stayed beside Alex during lunch. Conversations slowly returned. Students laughed again. Chairs scraped across the floor. Teachers continued lessons like nothing had happened.

Still—

Every few minutes, someone glanced toward Alex.

Sometimes nervously.

Sometimes curiously.

Alex didn’t notice.Or maybe he just didn’t care.

He sat near the window as usual, one hand resting against his cheek while staring absentmindedly at the cloudy sky outside. Sunlight flickered faintly through moving clouds, reflecting softly across his glasses.

The teacher continued explaining formulas across the board.

“...and if we substitute this value here—”

Alex wasn’t listening.

At all.

His notebook remained almost empty except for random pen marks near the corner of the page.

Ryan leaned slightly toward Ethan from behind.

“Bro’s spiritually somewhere else.”

Ethan snorted quietly.

Lara glanced toward Alex for a moment.

Even now, after everything earlier…

He looked tired more than dangerous.

Like his body was present in class while his mind was somewhere far away.

The teacher suddenly stopped writing.

Then slowly turned around.

“Alex.”

No response.

Ryan immediately whispered,“You’re dead.”

“Alex.”

This time, Alex blinked and looked away from the window.

The teacher pointed toward the board filled with equations.

“Since the sky seems more interesting than my class… would you like to solve question six?”

A few students laughed quietly.

Ryan leaned back dramatically.

“Yep. Funeral confirmed.”

Alex stared at the board for about two seconds.

Then stood up calmly.

No notebook.

No hesitation.

He walked toward the board while the classroom watched silently.

Even Lara looked curious now.

Alex grabbed the marker, glanced once at the equation—

—and immediately started solving.

Step after step.

Clean.

Fast.

Accurate.

The marker moved smoothly across the board without pause like he already knew the answer before reading the full question.

Within less than a minute—

He placed the marker down.

The entire problem was solved perfectly.

Silence.

The teacher blinked twice while checking the board.

“…correct.”

Ryan looked offended.

“Why does this idiot act like he failed every subject when he’s secretly a genius?”

Ethan smirked slightly.

Alex had already returned to his seat beside the window before the reactions even finished.

Like solving it meant absolutely nothing to him.

But Lara noticed something.

For a brief moment while writing—

his expression changed.

Focused.

Sharp.

Almost cold in a completely different way.

Not violent.

Just intelligent enough to feel dangerous.

And somehow…

that was equally intimidating.

The classes continued quietly afterward.

Then finally—

The last bell rang.

Instant chaos erupted across the classroom.

Students immediately packed their bags while conversations exploded around the room.

Ryan stretched dramatically.

“Freedom.”

Ethan grabbed his bag.

“You say that every single day.”

“Because it’s true every single day.”

Lara laughed softly while standing up from her seat.

Alex remained seated.

Still looking outside.

Then—

The classroom door opened again.

The principal’s secretary stepped inside holding a folder against her chest.

The room gradually quieted.

Her eyes scanned the classroom.

Then landed on him.

“Alex.”

He looked over lazily.

“You have detention after school hours.”

Silence.

Complete silence.

Several students genuinely looked shocked.

Even Ryan blinked.

“…detention?”

One of the students near the back whispered quietly,“Bro even Ethan never got detention…”

Ethan looked mildly offended.

“Why’s everyone using me as the school crime standard?”

Nobody answered him.

Because honestly—

they weren’t wrong.

Ethan had been suspended before.

Dragged into the office countless times.

But detention?

Never.

And now Alex had somehow become the first.

The secretary adjusted her glasses slightly.

“You’re expected in the disciplinary room immediately.”

Then she left.

The classroom exploded into whispers instantly.

Ryan stared at Alex dramatically.

“You’re cooked.”

“I’m aware.”

Ethan crossed his arms.

“Honestly? Could’ve been worse.”

Ryan looked at him.

“He literally almost killed someone.”

“…exactly,” Ethan replied.

Fair point.

Alex finally stood up and grabbed his bag casually.

Lara watched him carefully.

“You okay?”

Alex shrugged lightly.

“It’s detention. Not execution.”

Ryan pointed toward him immediately.

“See? This is why horror movie characters die first. Too calm.”

Alex ignored him and walked toward the door.

After a second—

Lara quietly followed behind him.

The disciplinary room near the administration block felt strangely quiet after school hours.

Most students had already gone home.

The orange evening sunlight stretched faintly across the hallway floors through the windows.

Alex sat alone near the desk inside the room, lazily holding his Nintendo Switch while waiting.

The secretary stood near a cabinet organizing files quietly.

Soft clicking sounds from the console filled the silence.

Ten minutes passed.

Then another five.

Alex didn’t seem bothered at all.

Until—

The door opened.

Alex looked up automatically.

“…Lara?”

She stepped inside holding her bag against her shoulder.

“You sound disappointed.”

“I was expecting the principal.”

“So was I.”

Alex blinked.

“Wait—you got detention too?”

“Amazing detective skills.”

Alex stared at her another second before lowering the Switch slowly.

“…why?”

Lara walked over and sat beside him.

“Apparently being present during violent incidents counts.”

“That’s stupid.”

“Probably.”

Alex frowned slightly.

“You didn’t do anything wrong.”

Lara looked at him quietly for a second.

Neither did you, technically.

But she didn’t say it aloud.

Instead she glanced toward the game screen.

“You’ve been playing the same boss for fifteen minutes.”

“It’s annoying.”

“You lost three times.”

“…you were watching?”

“You sigh very loudly.”

Alex looked mildly betrayed.

A small smile appeared on Lara’s face.

For a moment—

everything felt strangely peaceful again.

No whispers.

No blood.

No terrified faces.

Just quiet conversation beneath evening sunlight.

Then the door opened once more.

The principal finally entered carrying several folders under one arm.

He looked exhausted already.

“Sorry for the delay.”

Alex immediately put the Switch away.

The principal sat down across from them and adjusted his glasses.

“I considered suspension.”

Ryan’s voice echoed somewhere in Alex’s memory:You’re cooked.

“But,” the principal continued calmly, “after hearing the full situation… I decided against it.”

Alex stayed silent.

The principal opened one of the folders.

“Instead, both of you will be handling disciplinary service duties.”

Lara blinked slightly.

Alex raised an eyebrow.

“…meaning?”

The principal leaned back slightly.

“You both know the old library building near the east wing?”

Alex immediately frowned.

“The abandoned one?”

“Unused,” the principal corrected tiredly. “Not abandoned.”

“It literally has plants growing through the windows.”

“That is exactly the problem.”

Lara tried not to laugh.

The principal sighed.

“The school will be receiving more students next academic year. We may need that building operational again.”

He slid a paper across the table.

“For the next month, your responsibility will be restoring the library to usable condition after school hours.”

Alex stared at the paper.

Then the principal continued.

“After the new students arrive, you’ll both manage the library together for another month.”

Silence.

Then Alex looked up.

“…I understand my punishment.”

His eyes shifted toward Lara.

“But why her?”

Lara looked slightly surprised.

Alex continued calmly,

“I’m the reason the incident happened. She wasn’t involved.”

The principal’s expression softened slightly.

“She was present during the situation.”

“That’s not the same thing.”

“Alex,” Lara started quietly—

but he ignored her.

“She doesn’t deserve punishment for my actions.”

For a brief second—

the principal studied him carefully.

Not the violent student from earlier.

Not the boy everyone feared now.

Just someone carrying guilt heavier than he should.

Finally, the principal sighed.

“Alex. Wait outside for a moment.”

Alex frowned slightly but stood up anyway.

Before leaving, he glanced once toward Lara.

Then quietly walked out.

The door closed.

Silence settled inside the office.

The principal removed his glasses slowly and rubbed his eyes.

Lara immediately noticed the exhaustion on his face.

“…Dad?”

He stayed quiet for a few seconds before speaking.

“I spoke with the psychiatrist near the school yesterday.”

Lara’s expression changed slightly.

The principal leaned back in his chair.

“I wanted to understand what happened to Alex.”

The room felt quieter now.

“He told me Alex likely has unresolved trauma connected to harassment cases involving girls.”

Lara listened silently.

“But nobody knows exactly what happened.”

The principal’s jaw tightened slightly.

“And apparently… counseling sessions never went well.”

A pause.

“He shuts down.”

Lara slowly looked toward the closed door outside.

The principal followed her gaze.

“But today,” he said quietly, “you stopped him.”

Her eyes widened slightly.

“I saw it myself.”

His voice softened.

“When you called his name… he came back.”

Silence filled the room again.

Then he looked directly at her.

“So I’m asking you honestly.”

Not as principal.

As a father.

“Stay close to him.”

Lara blinked slowly.

“He doesn’t need punishment right now,” the principal continued quietly.

“He needs someone who can pull him back before he loses himself completely.”

For a moment—

she remembered Alex earlier.

Sitting near the window.

Laughing quietly during lunch.

Looking exhausted instead of dangerous.

Then she nodded softly.

“…okay.”

The principal exhaled slowly.

“Thank you.”

A few moments later—

Lara stepped outside the office.

Alex stood near the hallway window waiting quietly with his hands in his pockets.

He looked over immediately.

“…everything okay?”

Lara stared at him for a second before smiling faintly.

“Yeah.”

He studied her expression carefully.

Then nodded once.

The two of them began walking side by side through the nearly empty school hallways while evening sunlight stretched across the floors around them.

The hallway beyond the administration block had grown quieter by the time Alex and Lara stepped outside.

Most students had already gone home.

The evening sky burned orange through the tall school windows, long shadows stretching across the empty corridors as they walked side by side toward the east wing.

For a while, neither of them spoke.

The silence wasn’t awkward anymore.

Not after everything today.

Alex kept one hand inside his pocket while lazily spinning his Nintendo Switch cartridge with the other.

Lara noticed.

“You carry that everywhere?”

“It’s important emotional support.”

“That’s genuinely sad.”

“It’s extremely effective.”

A faint smile tugged at Lara’s lips.

Their footsteps echoed softly through the nearly empty building.

Then—

Alex slowed slightly.

Lara noticed immediately.

“…what?”

He didn’t answer at first.

His eyes remained fixed ahead.

The corridor leading toward the old library was no longer empty.

Several figures stood near the entrance.

Waiting.

Lara’s stomach tightened slightly.

Five students.

Maybe six.

All seniors.

And standing among them—

were the two boys from the storage room.

Bruises still visible across their faces.

One of them had a bandage wrapped across his nose.

The moment Alex recognized them—

the relaxed atmosphere vanished instantly.

Not violently.

Not dramatically.

It simply disappeared.

Like a switch turning off.

One of the injured boys pointed toward Alex slowly.

“…that’s him.”

The tallest senior standing near the center shifted his gaze toward Alex calmly.

Then toward Lara beside him.

His expression changed slightly.

Curious.

Careful.

The hallway suddenly felt much colder than before.

Lara glanced sideways toward Alex.

His face looked unreadable again.

Quiet.

Still.

Dangerously still.

The seniors slowly stepped away from the library entrance—

blocking the hallway completely.

Nobody spoke for a few seconds.

Only the sound of distant evening wind echoed faintly through the corridor windows.

Then the tallest senior finally spoke.

Low.Calm.

“So you’re Alex.”

Silence.

Lara felt the tension immediately.

Like the entire hallway was waiting for one wrong word.

Then—

Very slowly—

Alex took his hand out of his pocket.

And the boy with the bandaged nose instinctively flinched.
